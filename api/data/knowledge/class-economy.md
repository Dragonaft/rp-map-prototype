## No class is self-sufficient, by design

Each player class — **NOBLE**, **HOLY**, **GUILD** by default — has an exclusive building that
turns one raw resource into a unique prestige Good, arranged in a directed cycle:

**NOBLE → HOLY → GUILD → NOBLE**

| Class | Building | Input → Output | Elite unit | Elite unit eats (per turn) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NOBLE | Stud Farm | 50 grain → 20 Warhorses | Grand Host | Relics (from HOLY) |
| HOLY | Reliquary (needs Temple) | 20 gold → 20 Relics | Templar Order | Spices (from GUILD) |
| GUILD | Spice Wharf (needs Port) | 40 fish → 20 Spices | Free Company | Warhorses (from NOBLE) |

Every class's elite unit is **recruited** with Money or Piety plus a one-time cost in its own
class's prestige Good. Its **ongoing per-turn upkeep**, though, draws Food plus its *trading
partner's* prestige Good — not its own. That's the whole point: you can produce Warhorses all day
as NOBLE, but your Grand Host still needs Relics from a HOLY player to keep fighting.

## Why this matters

Losing access to your partner class's Goods — no trade route, no war spoils, hostile relations —
starves your elite Army through the normal Supply mechanic (see **Supply & Food**): an unfed
Army takes attrition every turn it goes without. This is deliberate pressure to trade or fight
rather than tech up in total isolation.

Everything below the elite tier — base buildings, base Troops, ordinary income — needs zero
prestige Goods. A player cut off from their partner class is never bricked, only capped below
the top tier.

## Gold is the tight bottleneck

Gold deposits are the rarest resource on the map (~4% of land Provinces), which makes HOLY's
Relic supply the tightest link in the whole ring — worth remembering whether you're HOLY yourself
or trying to starve one out.

## Classes aren't fixed

NOBLE/HOLY/GUILD are the default three, but classes are admin-configurable — a campaign could add
or hide one. Whatever's currently active shows up automatically in the Research tree's tabs; see
**Research & Classes** for how you pick a class in the first place.
