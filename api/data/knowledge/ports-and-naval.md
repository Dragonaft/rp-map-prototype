## Water Provinces can never be owned

They're pale blue on the map, have no landscape, and can't be claimed, built on, or roaded. Your
Armies can still travel across them and fight there — you just can't hold them like land.

## Getting an Army onto water

You need a **PORT** building. Embarking (moving from land to water) is only possible from a
Province where you currently have a PORT — this applies whether the move is a direct step or a
multi-hop road move, and it's checked against your Army's *actual* current Province.

PORT can only be built in a Province that itself neighbors a water Province.

Once at sea, moving between water Provinces is free — no PORT needed for water-to-water hops.
**Disembarking** (landing back on land) is unrestricted too: no PORT required on either end, land
onto any Province under the normal adjacency/combat/diplomacy rules.

## You can't stay at sea forever

An Army can spend up to **6 consecutive turns** on water before it's lost outright — Research can
extend this allowance. The counter resets to zero the moment the Army lands anywhere. Plan naval
campaigns around this: know your allowance and don't let a fleet drift past it.

## Fighting on water is different from fighting on land

Every Troop type fights at reduced strength at sea — Cavalry loses the most (roughly −80% of its
land power), Infantry and Ranged lose noticeably less. Check a Troop type's water combat modifier
before betting a campaign on it.

The bigger difference: **on water, the losing side of a battle is always wiped out completely** —
there's no partial-casualty retreat like there is on land. Losing a fight at sea means losing the
whole Army, every time.

## Fish and the Guild economy

PORT doesn't just enable naval movement — it also produces **Fish** every turn as a raw resource,
regardless of what the Province's own deposit is. Fish feeds the GUILD class's Spice Wharf (see
**The Class Economy**), so holding coastline matters even if you never send an Army to sea.
